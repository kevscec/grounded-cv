# Interview guide

A question bank organized by knowledge-base file. Work one block per pass. The follow-ups
exist to turn a vague answer into a gradeable one — ask them.

**Throughout:** after any achievement, ask the two questions that set the grade.
*"Where is that recorded?"* sets the tier. *"Who else worked on it?"* sets the attribution.

---

## Block 1 — Identity and facts

1. Full name as it should appear on a CV.
2. Current employer, official job title, and the title you would actually use externally.
   → *Follow-up:* do these differ? Many companies use internal titles that mean nothing
   outside. Record both; a CV may use the functional one.
3. Start date at each role. Month and year.
   → *Follow-up:* any internships, contracts or part-time periods immediately before? Do they
   belong as separate entries?
4. Education: institution, programme, status, dates. In progress counts — with an expected
   date it reads as a positive signal.
   → *Follow-up:* any incomplete studies? They can be included honestly or omitted; record the
   fact either way.
5. Certifications: issuer and date for each. Only ones actually held.
   → *Follow-up:* anything you use daily but are not certified in? That is a skill, not a
   certification. It goes in the skills section, never in certifications.
6. Contact details, and which links are current.
   → *Follow-up:* check every handle. A dead link on a CV is worse than no link.
7. Location, timezone, remote preference.
8. Work authorization: where can you legally work without sponsorship?
   → This is a hard filter on which postings are worth answering. Record it plainly.
9. Languages, with an honest level per skill. A certificate that tests only reading and
   listening does not establish speaking.

## Block 2 — Impact and numbers

1. What are you measured on? What does your employer count?
2. Name every number you can attach to your work — volume, accuracy, time, money, headcount,
   uptime, error rates, adoption.
   → *For each:* where is it recorded? Who produced it — you, a small group, or a project team?
3. What broke, and what did you do about it?
   → Incident and recovery stories are usually the strongest and most under-reported material.
4. What did you build that other people now depend on?
   → *Follow-up:* how many people, how often?
5. What did you make faster, cheaper, or more reliable? By how much, against what baseline?
6. Is there a number you are proud of that you cannot prove? Say so — it is still worth
   recording, graded C, so it can be used hedged rather than lost.

## Block 3 — Capabilities and technology

1. For each technology: what have you actually built with it, and what is the hardest problem
   you solved in it?
   → A capability without a story behind it is a keyword. Record the story.
2. Which of these could you be interviewed on for an hour without preparation?
   → That is the honest line between deep and familiar.
3. What do you get asked to help others with?
4. What is in your stack that you have only watched someone else use?
   → Record it as exposure. Never as experience.
5. **Honest absences:** what would a role require that you genuinely cannot do today?
   → This section is what makes a fit assessment possible. Push once, then record it.

## Block 4 — Projects

For each significant project:

1. What was the problem, in business terms?
2. What did you personally build or decide? What did others build?
3. What technologies, and what was non-obvious about using them here?
4. What was the outcome, and how is it known?
5. What went wrong, and what did you change afterwards?
6. Could you show this to an employer, or is it confidential?
   → Confidential work still belongs in the knowledge base. It gets translated before it
   reaches an external document.

## Block 5 — Recognition and external validation

1. Any awards, formal recognitions, promotions, or written praise?
   → *Follow-up:* who gave it, when, and for what? Recognition from several independent
   sources is far stronger than volume from one.
2. Is any of it public or shareable?
3. Were you part of a team that won something?
   → **Attribution matters here more than anywhere.** Team recognition is real and worth
   claiming — as team recognition.

## Block 6 — Ways of working, and the differentiator

1. How do you decide what to build versus what to leave alone?
2. What do you do that colleagues at your level typically do not?
   → This is the differentiator question. Most people answer it badly on the first try; ask it
   twice, in different words.
3. What do you do after something ships?
4. How do you work with AI tools, concretely?
   → For some roles this is now a top differentiator and for others it is noise. Record it in
   detail; decide audience by audience.

## Block 7 — Targeting

1. What roles are you aiming at? What would you take, and what would you refuse?
2. Seniority target.
3. Compensation: current and target. **For targeting decisions only — never printed on a CV.**
4. Company type preference: product, consultancy, enterprise, startup.
5. Anything you want excluded — industries, companies, arrangements.
