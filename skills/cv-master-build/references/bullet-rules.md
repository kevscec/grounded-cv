# Bullet rules

## The shape

The industry standard is the XYZ formula, attributed to Laszlo Bock (former SVP of People
Operations at Google):

> **Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z].**

- **X** — the outcome. What changed, not the task you were assigned.
- **Y** — the number that proves it.
- **Z** — the specific decision or method that produced it.

It works because a recruiter's first pass is a few seconds long, and this delivers a result, a
number and a method in one glance.

**Word order matters more than the letters.** Lead with the verb and the outcome; the method
follows.

> *Eliminated a recurring out-of-memory crash class by root-causing 108 crashes to a fixed heap
> request and replacing it with a dynamic formula.*

beats the same facts told method-first.

## The honest amendment

Not every strong bullet has a number. Architectural decisions — moving logic out of a model into
deterministic code, choosing not to automate something — are often the most differentiating
material a person has, and forcing a fabricated metric onto them is worse than omitting one.

**The rule: a bullet needs either a number or a non-obvious engineering decision.** A bullet
with neither is a job description. Cut it.

## Verb bank

Lead with one. Never repeat a verb inside a section.

**Ownership and creation** — Architected · Built · Designed · Engineered · Shipped · Originated ·
Established · Owned · Led

**Change and improvement** — Eliminated · Reduced · Migrated · Re-architected · Replaced ·
Standardized · Consolidated · Extended · Optimized

**Analysis and rigor** — Root-caused · Diagnosed · Reconciled · Validated · Instrumented ·
Mined · Audited · Measured

**Reach and enablement** — Trained · Enabled · Unblocked · Documented · Advised

**Attribution-safe forms for team results** — the only permitted openings:
*Contributed to an initiative that…* · *Part of the team that…* · *Co-owned…* ·
*Jointly delivered…*

## Banned constructions

| Never write | Why |
|---|---|
| "Responsible for…" / "Duties included…" | A job description, not an accomplishment |
| "Familiar with…" / "Exposure to…" / "Working knowledge of…" | Reads as a bluff. Either it is a skill or it is not on the CV |
| "Results-driven", "detail-oriented", "team player", "passionate about" | Unfalsifiable. Costs a line, buys nothing |
| "Utilized", "leveraged", "spearheaded" | Thesaurus verbs. Use *used*, *built*, *led* |
| Three metrics stacked in one bullet | One idea and one number per line, or none of them land |
| First-person pronouns | CV bullets start with a verb |
| Model names as skills (GPT-x, Claude, Gemini) | Hiring sources call this out explicitly — the role cares about system design, not which API was called |
| "Prompt engineering" with no production or evaluation evidence | Reads as research, not engineering |
| A team metric with an individual verb | The one unforgivable error. See the attribution rule |

## Sole-actor framing

A subtler failure than an outright false claim, and more common: writing collaborative work as
though it were solo.

> *"I found where AI could help and pushed it through"* — when leadership backed it, a review
> board approved it, and a platform team built the runtime.

It is not technically false and it will still read as dishonest to anyone who was there. Credit
what was real. It costs nothing and it is the difference between a CV a former colleague would
endorse and one that would embarrass them.

## Register

A CV is seen only by the target employer, so it runs at full strength — "Engineer" rather than
"Developer" where both are defensible, achievement-dense phrasing, strong ownership verbs
wherever the evidence supports them.

**What full register does not change:** the tier system, the attribution rule, and the redlines.
Leading with impact is not the same as loosening truth. A team number keeps its qualifier on a
CV exactly as it does anywhere else.
